## About

Select a word, phrase or passage to see translations, definitions, images and AI explanations in a popup beside the text. Switch between sources and languages, then carry on reading.

Definer works as a browser extension and a desktop app for Windows and Linux. Use it for language learning, vocabulary building and research, with **History** to revisit your lookups and **Collections** to keep useful results.

![Definer's Bubble beside selected text, showing a definition, pronunciation, example sentence, synonyms and alternative sources](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/getting-started/bubble-selected-word.png)

## ⭐ Key features

- **Translate in context.** Understand words, idioms and whole passages with help from the surrounding text.
- **Look deeper.** Get definitions, usage examples, synonyms, pronunciation, encyclopedia articles and image results.
- **Ask AI.** Explain a difficult sentence, explore a concept or ask a follow-up question. Choose hosted or local models and write reusable prompts.
- **Read across formats.** Use the extension on webpages, open PDFs and EPUBs in Reader, or use Desktop's Word and Region tools.
- **Reopen past lookups.** Browse History, revisit source results and review their available reading context.
- **Keep useful material.** Organize editable collections and export to Anki, CSV, JSON or Markdown.
- **Bring your own sources.** Connect websites, import offline dictionaries, or build an interactive source with JavaScript.
- **Automate repetitive work.** Use Routines to interact with website results and Recipes to turn source data into saved items.
- **Make it yours.** Customize languages, activation shortcuts, fonts, colors, layouts and themes, with separate settings profiles.

## 🌍 Understand what you read

In the browser, select text and click the small icon beside it to open the **Bubble**. You can also enable double-click activation, configure a key to hold while selecting text, use a keyboard shortcut, or look up a selection from the right-click menu. Type a query from the extension toolbar when you want to start a search yourself.

### Contextual translation

A phrase can mean something very different from its individual words. Select it in a webpage and **Lum Translator** can use the surrounding sentence or paragraph to help choose the translation. Its **In Context** view shows the translated phrase within the sentence, so you can see how it fits. Alternative translations, transliteration and reverse meanings help you explore the wording further.

[![Contextual translation of the Spanish idiom echar una mano as lend a hand, highlighted inside the translated sentence, with alternatives and other sources available](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/contextual-translation.png)](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/contextual-translation.png)

You can also select and translate a longer passage. Set preferred languages, change the translation direction, and choose which parts of the result to display. The interface language and the languages used by your sources are separate choices: you can read an explanation in one language and translate into another.

### Dictionaries, pronunciation and research

**Lumtionary** provides multilingual definitions, examples, phonetic transcriptions, synonyms and antonyms. Explore the different meanings of a word, hear it pronounced, or look up an entire expression.

Add **YouGlish** to hear words and phrases spoken in videos, with examples of natural pronunciation and usage.

A **source** is one of the tools that answers your lookup. Switch sources to read a Wikipedia article, inspect Wikidata facts, search for images, or consult another dictionary. Content sources and pronunciation sources are configured separately, so you can choose both where answers come from and how text is spoken. Enable the sources you use, arrange their order, and adjust their individual settings.

### Languages that fit your reading

Choose and prioritize your result languages, then switch languages directly in the Bubble. Definer can follow your priority order and try the next language when a source has no result, remember a separate choice for each source, or remember your choice across sources using the shared language list.

Keep definitions in the language you're studying and translations in a familiar language. Sources with their own language controls retain their individual choices.

[![Languages settings with an ordered list of preferred languages and the Auto, Per-source and Global selection modes](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/languages.png)](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/languages.png)

### AI with your choice of model

Ask why a sentence uses a particular tense, request a simpler explanation of a technical term, or continue with follow-up questions. Reusable prompts let you give the AI instructions suited to your work, such as explaining an idiom, breaking down a sentence or discussing a selected passage.

Use Lumetrium's built-in service, connect a provider with your own API key, or connect to a local model through **Ollama** or **LM Studio**. Provider options include OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, OpenRouter and xAI, along with custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints. Prompts can have their own provider and model settings.

## 🕘 Return to your lookups in History

You don't have to stop reading to make a flashcard or write a note. **History → Lookups** keeps recorded lookups together with their source results, languages and available page context, so you can come back and decide what to keep later.

Browse the timeline or search by query. Page titles, source icons and dates help you recognize a word from an article, an explanation from a paper, or a phrase from yesterday's reading. Open the original page directly from its History row.

[![Definer Options, History → Lookups: a timeline of translations, dictionary entries, AI answers, image searches and other lookups with distinct page favicons, source icons, dates and save actions](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/history-lookups.png)](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/history-lookups.png)

### Reopen the results and their context

Expand a lookup to see its retained results beside its recorded details. Switch between sources, read the original sentence or paragraph when available, and see where the selected text appeared. The **Overview** brings the recorded context, saving options and previously saved items together.

Here, a lookup of “curiosity” retains the article's title and link, the sentence where the word appeared, and the available source results. The saved vocabulary item remains attached to that lookup.

[![Expanded browser History lookup with its Page card and favicon, original sentence, dictionary result, other source choices, Recipe and saved item](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/history-expanded.png)](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/history-expanded.png)

[View the expanded lookup at full size](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/history-expanded.png) to inspect the recorded context, results and saving controls.

Save from an older result just as you would while reading. Choose a [Recipe](#decide-what-gets-saved-with-recipes)—a template for the item you want to create—and a destination Collection. A learner can turn an expression into a study card; a researcher can combine an explanation, context and page link in a note. **History → Saves** brings together items created from lookups across your Collections.

The Bubble also has a **Recent lookups** menu for quickly returning to something you just opened. Cloud History makes recorded lookups available across signed-in devices.

## 🗂️ Organize and use what you keep

### Collections

**Collections** hold material you choose to keep and edit. Organize vocabulary, study cards, research notes and other saved content with tags and categories, or create items yourself. Switch between layouts and choose the fields that matter to your collection.

Saved items can link back to their original lookup, so you can revisit the context and other results as you review them.

[![Browser Collections showing saved study cards and vocabulary entries with definitions, examples, tags and categories](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/collections.png)](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/collections.png)

### Decide what gets saved with Recipes

A **Recipe** is a reusable set of instructions for turning source data into a collection item. It chooses the content and how it is arranged: a word, pronunciation, definition and example sentence could become a vocabulary entry; an explanation, selected context and original page link could become a research note.

Start with the built-in Recipes, then make copies to adapt in the visual editor or JSON editor. **Liquid templates** let you map and format the available data, including data collected by Routines or produced by Script sources. A custom Recipe can combine retained results from several sources into one item; running several Recipes creates separate items. Preview the result as you edit.

### Export to your study and writing tools

Send items to **Anki** through AnkiConnect, or export **CSV, JSON and Markdown** for other tools. Save a result and send it to Anki directly from the Bubble. Anki export can include supported media such as pronunciation audio; it uses a running desktop Anki installation with the AnkiConnect add-on.

## 🖥️ Beyond the browser

**Definer Desktop** brings dictionaries, translation and other sources to text in desktop apps, screenshots, scanned documents and games. Open its overlay toolbar and use **Word** to point at a word or **Region** to select an area of text. OCR recognizes the text and opens the Bubble beside it.

Use Word for a quick meaning while reading, or Region for dialogue, instructions and longer passages. The result can be translated, explained by AI, explored through another source, or saved for later. A separate **Search window** lets you type a query directly.

![Definer Desktop recognizing a selected Spanish passage and displaying its English translation, with six source icons available](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/store-listings/2026-10-23-reset-r02/library/en-us/main/96-region-final-dark.png)

Desktop includes Definer's source settings, History, Collections and appearance controls. The browser extension remains useful for direct text selection and webpage context; Desktop adds screen recognition across your other apps.

[Desktop downloads and demonstration](https://lumetrium.com/definer/desktop/)

<a id="reader"></a>

## 📚 Read PDFs and EPUBs

Open a PDF or EPUB in Definer's built-in **Reader** and use the Bubble directly in the document. Add files from your computer or a direct web address, keep them in a local library, and continue reading from your saved position.

Reader includes PDF annotations and EPUB chapter navigation and text-size controls. It is useful for language learning with books, studying papers, or keeping technical documentation close at hand. Desktop's OCR also works with scanned pages in your preferred document viewer.

## 🧩 Bring your own sources

### Add a website

Make a dictionary, search engine or specialist website part of your lookup. A **Custom source** uses the site's search URL to open results for your selected text inside Definer. Insert a lookup-text variable where the search term goes, then reuse that source for other words and phrases.

Add CSS to adjust the embedded page's colors, typography and layout. More advanced templates can use the lookup language and available page context. The settings page includes a live preview, so you can try a query while configuring the source.

[![Custom source settings with a Wiktionary URL template, CSS and a live dictionary preview](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/custom-source.png)](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/custom-source.png)

[Custom source guide](https://lumetrium.com/definer/wiki/sources/custom/)

### Upload your own dictionaries

Import dictionary packages and search them locally through the **Offline Dictionary** source. Keep a specialist glossary, a bilingual dictionary or a collection of dictionaries available alongside your other sources, with no network connection needed for those lookups.

Supported formats include **Yomitan term dictionaries, StarDict, ABBYY Lingvo DSL, MDict 2.0, Babylon, Aard 2 and ZIM 6.1–6.3**. Import files, folders or supported archives, choose a dictionary, and adjust matching and display settings. Use the dictionary's own colors or adapt its entries to your Definer theme.

### Build a source with JavaScript

A **Script source** turns a lookup into a small tool of your own. Read the selected text and available page context, fetch data from an API, and render the result with HTML and CSS. Add buttons and inputs, stream a response as it arrives, or preserve structured data with the result for saving later.

The script catalog includes a unit converter, JSON formatter, page citation generator, dictionary API client and local Ollama explainer. Use them as starting points for your own sources.

The editor offers API completion and supports top-level `await`. Scripts can also copy text, open links, and act on supported editable text selections through Definer's API.

JavaScript can turn a selected distance into a unit converter. The editor and live preview stay side by side; this example calculates the result, retains structured values and provides a **Copy result** button.

[![Script source editor with working JavaScript beside its live conversion of 10 kilometres into 6.21 miles, metres and feet](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/script-source.png)](https://media.lumetrium.com/definer/github-readme/v2/script-source.png)

## ⚡ Automate website results with Routines

When using a website as a source, you may want to open a particular section, remove distractions or collect specific text every time. A **Routine** performs those steps for you inside supported source results.

Build a sequence in the visual editor or edit its JSON. Steps can wait for content, click, scroll, type, extract data, remove elements, and repeat actions over a list. Run a Routine automatically when results arrive or trigger it manually.

For example, a Routine can extract a definition and example sentence from a dictionary website. That collected data can then be used by a Recipe to create a study card. The editor lets you test the sequence and inspect the extracted data while building it.

## 🎨 Make Definer fit the way you work

Choose a built-in theme or create your own with the **Theme Editor**. Change colors, fonts and corner roundness, or add custom CSS. Your theme carries through Definer's lookup and Options surfaces.

The **Theme Schedule Editor** lets you place themes on a daily timeline. Use a light theme during the day, switch to dark in the evening, or build a schedule around your preferred colors.

Download a source JSON file, then open **Definer Options → Sources → New source → Constructor** and choose the file. Review the imported Script before enabling it. Script is experimental; available source and Recipe limits still apply. [Source import guide](https://lumetrium.com/definer/wiki/sources/import-export/)

Lookup Notes keeps its note with that source result and language. Reopening it requires the lookup to be retained in History. Its included **Save lookup note** Recipe can also create a Collection Note. Page Citation uses available page metadata; Dictionary API needs an internet connection and provides English definitions. Context Inspector displays page text locally—review it before including anything in an issue.

For the AI Source, try [Collocations](prompts/definer-ai-prompt-collocations.json), [Conjugation](prompts/definer-ai-prompt-conjugation.json), or [Mnemonic](prompts/definer-ai-prompt-mnemonic.json). Add a downloaded file through **AI → Settings → Prompts → New prompt → Import File**. These prompts use **Lumetrium AI · Fast** and your available AI & Translation allowance. Their [Liquid templates](src/prompts/) are also editable. [Prompt guide](https://lumetrium.com/definer/wiki/sources/ai/prompts/)

Contributions to these scripts and prompts are welcome. Include an example input, the expected result, and a screenshot when it helps explain the change. Feature instructions and API details remain in the Wiki.
